Production modular geography SPRITE ATLAS for an illustrated medieval island simulation. Style reference: the attached painterly island; use only its hand-painted geological appearance, warm olive grass, angular blue-gray limestone, cream sand and turquoise shallows. Never photorealistic material swatches. Fixed coherent oblique three-quarter camera, about 35 degrees above ground, looking at the SOUTH and EAST faces; north always points upper-right and east lower-right. Consistent sun from upper-left. TRANSPARENT background. Exactly SIX distinct, completely separated terrain chunks arranged in a STRICT 3 columns x 2 rows layout, equal-size cells, generous transparent padding, every chunk entirely inside its own cell with no overlap, ground contact baseline consistent. No grid lines, captions, borders, UI, trees, vegetation props, buildings, roads, bridges or people. Each chunk is natural terrain, NOT a voxel cube, NOT a floating rectangular slab, NOT a whole island. The land top surfaces must meet the same elevation and style at shared logical diamond-grid edges, while the exposed shore follows a natural irregular curve. Terrain should form continuous land when neighbors overlap slightly; no decorative grass fringe around joining interior edges. Variation in size means some pieces occupy one diamond and others two or four neighboring diamonds. All six chunks must use identical camera and lighting. RELIEF AND WATERWAY MODULES SHEET. Row 1 left to right: (1) a LARGE four-diamond mountain massif with three linked snowy alpine peaks, connected ridges, a low valley through its foothills and broad geological base; (2) a smaller two-diamond rugged green rocky hill with no snow, clearly lower/smaller than the snowy massif; (3) a one-diamond small rocky knoll, clearly smaller and lower than the hill. Row 2 left to right: (4) an elongated two-diamond grassland river section running from rear to front, gently meandering turquoise freshwater channel, grass joins left/right; (5) a four-diamond river bend curving from rear-left to front-right with low grassy banks, continuous channel at the connection ends; (6) an elongated two-diamond rocky river gorge where upstream high water drops in one natural waterfall to a lower pool and downstream channel, solid continuous terrain around the water. Water follows continuous organic curves, no stepped pixel edges. Mountain massif must have a materially larger footprint than the hill, and hill larger than knoll. Terrain chunks, not buildings or isolated decorative objects.
